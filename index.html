Scholar-Athlete

The dream of the podium demands more than talent.

Across The Gambia, Ghana, and the world, young people see themselves playing for their country at the African Cup of Nations, the World Cup, the Olympic Games, World Championships, and other global sporting stages. Yet many are not willing to do everything that needs to be done to ensure their success.

Dr. Drammeh’s vision and strategy for the coming generations is the scholar-athlete: discipline in the classroom, discipline in training, and mentors who raise expectations. His own athletic career shows what that path can achieve.

Athletic profile and career

Selected and served as Team Captain of both his high school and university track and field programs.

  1. Three-time high school national champion.
  2. Gambia long jump national record holder.
  3. International Olympic Solidarity Scholarship recipient and the first Gambian to receive this scholarship (1995).
  4. Member of The Gambia team at the Centennial Olympic Games (Atlanta 1996) and qualifier for the Sydney 2000 Games.
  5. Full scholarship recipient to SC State University (1997).
  6. Six-time NCAA MEAC Conference long jump champion (1997–2000).
  7. MEAC Outdoor Conference Championships runner-up (2000).
  8. SC State University long jump record holder, indoor (7.69 m) and outdoor (7.87 m).
  9. NCAA Track and Field Consensus All-American (1999).
  10. SC State University Athletics Hall of Fame inductee (2012).
  11. MEAC Hall of Fame inductee (2021).

Changing the mindset

Many people hold the wrong perception that athletes are lazy or not intelligent. Lack of discipline, mentoring, and hard work feeds that thinking.

Dr. Drammeh’s mentoring program was created to challenge that mindset and the low expectations placed on our children. This profile is meant to serve as an inspirational nudge for every scholar-athlete.

Because of discipline, drive, determination, and courage, Drammeh became one of the most distinguished alumni and decorated scholar-athletes in the history of SC State University.

Helping young people learn, lead and contribute.

A phased digital platform for children and young people, built around personal development, character, citizenship, education, creativity, and service.

The guiding approach

Start small. Learn from young people. Build responsibly. The platform can begin with useful resources and activities, then grow into mentoring, competitions, projects, and partnerships.

Mentoring

Support critical thinking, collaboration, effective communication, and subject-matter competence through structured mentoring resources.

Education and AI

Introduce age-appropriate learning resources and responsible AI literacy, with guidance on safety, privacy, verification, and human judgement.

Community service
